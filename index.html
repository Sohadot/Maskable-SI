MASKABLE SUPERINTELLIGENCE · AN INDEPENDENT CONCEPT

Possession is
not permission.

Advanced intelligence may be capable of far more than it should be authorized to do. Maskable.si explores a future-facing principle: externally available capabilities should be scoped by explicit policy, independently evaluated and accountable to human authority.

01 — THESIS

Capability is a fact.
Permission is a decision.

THE ASSUMPTION

If a system can, it may.

Capability and authorization collapse into one surface. Every accessible tool or external action becomes a potential route to impact.

THE MASKABLE PROPOSITION

Every external capability has a boundary.

Tool access, operational permissions and delegated actions are evaluated against a defined authority and context—without claiming control over the model's internal knowledge.

WHY THE DISTINCTION MATTERS

More capable AI introduces more consequential decisions about deployment, tool use and autonomy. The strategic question is not whether an advanced model possesses a capacity, but whether a particular operator, task and environment should expose it. A useful control architecture must be measurable and resistant to bypass—not merely described in policy.

02 — MODEL

Grant. Enforce. Verify.

Three proposed design obligations. Each depends on evidence beyond the concept itself.

01 / POLICY DEFINITION

Grant

Specify which external capabilities a given task, user and environment may request. Establish who can approve or revoke a permission and why.

Authority is explicit
02 / RUNTIME BOUNDARY

Enforce

Make unavailable tools and actions unreachable at the execution boundary. Interface restrictions alone are not a substitute for robust enforcement.

Controls must be implemented
03 / INDEPENDENT EVALUATION

Verify

Test bypass attempts and failure modes, record evidence and reassess safeguards as systems and capabilities change.

Claims must be tested

PUBLIC REFERENCE / VERSION 0.1

Give the boundary
a definition.

Maskability is a proposed, testable property of an AI deployment's external action boundary: the ability to scope, enforce, revoke and examine permissions independently of what the model may know or infer.

Read the operational definition, trust assumptions and executable reference cases. The specification is a research proposal—not an adopted standard, a deployed control plane or an evaluated safety guarantee.

04 — SCALE

Maskability levels

A proposed vocabulary for describing how selectively an AI deployment exposes its external capabilities. These levels are not a validated maturity score or an industry standard.

M0

Unbounded

No deliberate capability boundary is expressed in this model.

M1

Coarse

Whole groups of tools are enabled or withheld together.

M2

Granular

Individual tools and action paths receive distinct permissions.

M3

Contextual

Exposure changes with task, environment, role and approval.

M4

Governed

Changes to capability boundaries are logged, reviewed, independently evaluated and subject to revocation. Effectiveness still requires real-world evidence.

Conceptual classification by Maskable.si, independent of any third-party certification or standard-setting body.
