MASKABLE / SUPERINTELLIGENCE / CONCEPT 2026

Superintelligence.
With boundaries.

The defining question of advanced intelligence may not be how much it can do, but what it is allowed to do, when, and for whom. Maskable.si positions maskability as a future design principle: powerful AI capabilities exposed only within deliberate, testable limits.

01 / THE THESISWHY THE BOUNDARY MATTERS

Intelligence may scale without limit. Authority must not.

Today’s frontier-AI research increasingly examines dangerous capabilities, misuse safeguards and human oversight. More capable systems introduce a structural problem: technical ability does not confer permission to act.

Maskable proposes a distinct product thesis: treat the exposure of capabilities as an explicit governance decision—defined by task, operating context, risk and accountable human authority.

02 / REFERENCE FRAMEWORKA PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE

Power is not permission.
Make capability conditional.

Five proposed controls define maskability as a property of an advanced AI deployment: which capabilities are exposed, under what authority, and with what checks.

01 MAP

Map the capability surface.

Identify the tools, access paths, autonomy levels and action channels a system could invoke.

02 AUTHORIZE

Establish who decides.

Bind available functions to purpose, responsible operators, environment and explicit authority.

03 MASK

Expose a smaller envelope.

Withhold tools, interfaces or actions that fall outside the context’s approved scope.

04 EVALUATE

Test the actual boundary.

Assess bypasses, misuse scenarios, failure modes and control effectiveness under stress.

05 GOVERN

Keep a human authority chain.

Define review, escalation, revocation and accountability before critical actions occur.

✳ Masking a tool or permission is not the same as suppressing a model’s internal knowledge or guaranteeing safe behavior. This is a proposed interface-and-governance principle, not proven control of superintelligence.
