01 / THE THESISWHY THE BOUNDARY MATTERS

The next intelligence stack has a disclosure problem.

AI agents, model gateways, analytics pipelines, and external collaborators increasingly consume information outside its original context. The challenge is no longer simply whether data is stored securely. It is whether each disclosure is justified.

Maskable proposes a simple architectural question at every boundary: What does this destination actually need to know?

02 / REFERENCE FRAMEWORKA PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE

Control the disclosure.
Preserve the utility.

Five conceptual controls describe how sensitive information could be handled before it enters a downstream workflow. They are design principles, not claims of an implemented product.

01 RECOGNIZE

Discover what matters.

Identify direct identifiers, credentials, quasi-identifiers, and context-dependent sensitive fields.

02 AUTHORIZE

Bind use to purpose.

Evaluate the request, recipient, environment, and permitted disclosure—not just the field type.

03 TRANSFORM

Reveal only enough.

Choose suppression, replacement, generalization, or other proportionate treatments.

04 VALIDATE

Test what remains.

Evaluate residual disclosure risk alongside the utility of the transformed information.

05 ACCOUNT

Make decisions explainable.

Record policy intent, review paths, exceptions, and evidence required for governance.

✳ These controls are an original conceptual synthesis informed by the sources below—not an issued technical standard or evidence of certification.
